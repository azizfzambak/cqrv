## 1. Summary

**What the reviews did not find.** No mathematical error and no gap in a proof. R1 and R3 recomputed
121/128, 7/128, ε ≈ 0.168426 and δ ≈ 0.189431 and the Table 4 totals, and found them consistent.
R3 reports a source build of the 62 modules of the `cqr` executable on Linux with no error or
warning; this is the reviewer's own report and is not repeated in the paper.

**What they found.**
- **Claim calibration.** The "general MBU theorem" is general in its compute block and its correction,
  but in this IR it applies only to quadratic functions, because well-formedness makes every
  classically controlled gate Clifford. The Khattar–Gidney result is correctness for every n, with the
  cost decided for n = 4…12 only. The 8n − 24 bound is a counting argument inside its cost model.
- **Related work.** The introduction's claim that verified compilers state only unitary facts is
  contradicted by the certified Shor implementation [Peng et al. 2023]; Lean-QuantumAlg (2026),
  Gleipnir (2021), the MBU lemma of Luongo et al. (2025) and the DeepMind / Google Quantum AI work
  of Shirobokov et al. (2026) were missing.
- **Prose against Lean.** Several theorem statements in the text omit hypotheses or cite the exact
  form where the Lean development also has the general one.
- **A trust-model gap.** The list of device qubits holding the data at the final layout (`dataPhys`)
  is a literal from the transpile tool. It selects the readout terms of δ′ and enters `PhysGrade`,
  and the kernel does not check it against the witness's final layout. Table 1 did not say so.
- **Factual slips.** README certificate count, CI step count, a CI-logs claim, the idle diagnostic, a
  threshold typo, a cross-reference.
